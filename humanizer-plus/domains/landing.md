# Landing page
Applies to: landing pages, marketing sections, product and studio sites, ads and app-store listings.
Not for: in-product strings (`ui-copy`), social posts (`social`), case-study articles (`article`).

## Reader and purpose

A visitor deciding in seconds whether this is for them. The page says what they get, proves it with facts from the brief, and gives one next step. This card covers the words; visual direction comes from the design skills, and the brand's own guide wins on tone, casing and CTA style.

## Structure

- The headline says what the visitor gets, in a collocation a reader would say aloud.
- Each benefit is a verb phrase with a concrete object taken from the brief.
- One clear CTA verb per section.
- Section labels may follow the vertical's conventions (real estate: overview, location, amenities, floor plans, legal status, sales policy).
- Keep every condition, fee, deadline and channel next to the offer it limits.

## Length and format

- Sentence case by default; ALL CAPS only where the brand guide uses it.
- Prices follow vi.md section 5 (*2,5 tỷ đồng, 89.000đ*).
- English is the default language for pages aimed at international or freelance clients; screenshots there must not show another language's UI.

## Medium tells

- The name-swap test (VI-24): if another brand's name fits the copy unchanged, the copy lacks facts. Ask for them rather than inventing.
- Two nominal slogans in a row read as a template (VI-06). A rejected headline is a lesson about that collocation in that role, not a ban on its words; alternatives a reviewer offers are directions, not approved copy.
- Unproven rankings and invented social proof. Replace an unproven ranking with a countable fact from the brief (VI-23); when the client says proof exists, ask for the document rather than deleting the claim. Never invent a number or a testimonial.
- Naming the builder's customers or any client company in public copy; describe them by sector.
- English vocabulary is covered by [upstream §12 Overused AI words] and [upstream §16 Sales language]: no "unlock", "seamless", "elevate", "in the heart of", "cutting-edge".

## Vietnamese notes

- Pick one row of the vi.md register matrix and hold it in every section: *bạn* for SaaS, studios, dev tools and consumer apps; *quý khách* for banks, insurance, real estate and hospitality. The company is *chúng tôi*; *chúng ta* only when the visitor is really included (VI-40).
- Hán-Việt prestige words (*kiến tạo, an cư, tinh hoa, thịnh vượng*) belong to luxury and real-estate copy. SaaS and studio copy uses plain words (*làm, dùng, xem, tạo*). A page that mixes the two reads off.
- Cut agency boilerplate: *giải pháp toàn diện, nâng tầm thương hiệu, bộ mặt thương hiệu, kỷ nguyên số, tối ưu hóa hiệu quả* (VI-20).
- CTAs are verb plus object, optionally *ngay*: *Dùng thử miễn phí, Đặt lịch demo, Nhận báo giá, Xem chi tiết, Tìm hiểu thêm, Liên hệ, Đăng ký nhận tin*. Replace calques: *Học thêm, Đọc nhiều hơn, Lấy báo giá, Liên lạc chúng tôi, Nhấp vào đây, Đừng bỏ lỡ cơ hội này* (VI-16).
- Luật Quảng cáo 16/2012/QH13, Điều 8 khoản 11 allows *nhất, duy nhất, tốt nhất, số một* and equivalents (*No.1, #1, hàng đầu*) only with documented proof.
- Lint with `--doctype marketing`.

## Review checklist

- Does the headline tell a stranger what they get, in words they would say?
- Is the address the same in the hero, the FAQ and the form?
- Does every claim trace to the brief, and every superlative to proof?
- Would the copy still fit if another brand's name were swapped in?
