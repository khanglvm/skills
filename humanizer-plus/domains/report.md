# Report
Applies to: reports, research write-ups, analyses, audits, decision memos, postmortems and knowledge-base concept pages.
Not for: blog posts and features (`article`), step-by-step docs (`technical-docs`), short status updates (`workplace`).

## Reader and purpose

Someone who must decide or act on the findings, often without time to read the evidence. They need the answer first, then how sure it is, then enough evidence to trust or challenge it.

## Structure

- Open with the answer or recommendation and the decision it supports, in two or three sentences.
- Then the findings, most important first, each with its evidence and how confident the writer is.
- Then what is unknown or was not checked, and what would change the conclusion.
- Then next steps with owners, when the brief gives them.
- Method and sources go last or in an appendix, as links the reader can follow.
- A postmortem keeps the timeline factual (what happened, when, who noticed) and keeps blame out of it.

## Length and format

- Headings name the finding ("Most failures come from expired tokens"), not the topic ("Token analysis").
- Tables for comparisons the reader will scan; prose for reasoning.
- Every number has its unit, date range and source. Rounded numbers say so.
- A knowledge-base page states the current fact or rule and links its evidence; the history of how it was found stays in the record, not the page.

## Medium tells

- An executive summary that announces what the report will cover instead of what it found ([upstream §4 Staged run-up before the point]).
- Method narration in the body: "We searched X, then reviewed Y" ([upstream §25 Writing about the document instead of its subject]).
- Every finding hedged to the same degree, so the reader cannot tell strong from weak ([upstream §9 Stacked qualifiers]). State the confidence each one has.
- A "Challenges and future outlook" section with no facts in it ([upstream §13 Inflated significance]).
- Recommendations that follow from no finding in the report.

## Vietnamese notes

- Impersonal register: the system, the data or the team is the subject; no *tôi* outside a signed opinion (vi.md section 4).
- Numbers and dates follow vi.md section 5; tables use full digits, prose uses scale words (*2,5 tỷ đồng*).
- Keep legal, medical and administrative Hán-Việt terms as they are (vi.md section 6).
- Confidence words keep their exact weight: *có thể, chưa, chỉ, vẫn* survive every rewrite.

## Review checklist

- Can a reader act on the first paragraph alone?
- Does every finding carry its evidence and a confidence level?
- Is every number traceable to a source, with unit and period?
- Are the gaps and unchecked areas stated, rather than hidden?
