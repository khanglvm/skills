# Social post
Applies to: posts and threads on LinkedIn, X, Facebook, Threads and community forums, plus short announcements shared there.
Not for: long-form articles (`article`), landing and ad copy (`landing`), team chat (`workplace`).

## Reader and purpose

Someone scrolling past dozens of posts. The first line decides whether they stop, and the post earns the stop by saying something specific: what happened, what was made, what was learned, with the detail only the writer has.

## Structure

- First line: the finding, the result or the news, in plain words.
- Then the one detail that makes it real: a number, a date, a name the writer gave, a link.
- A thread splits where the subject changes, not every two sentences; each post must make sense when quoted alone.
- End on the last fact, the link or a real question to the reader.

## Length and format

- Follow the platform's limit and the writer's own past posts for length.
- Line breaks between ideas, not between every sentence.
- Hashtags only where the writer's sample uses them, at the end.
- Emoji only at the writer's own rate; none as bullets or section icons.
- Links and handles exactly as given.

## Medium tells

- The confession arc: "After X years of doing Y, I realized..." followed by a lesson that fits anyone.
- Staccato slogan fragments in a row ("One standard. Every AI. Every API.") ([upstream §2 One-line closers and dramatic fragments]).
- Engagement bait at the end ("Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Save this for later") when no real question is being asked.
- A numbered list of generic lessons with a bold label on every line ([upstream §19 Bold as decoration]).
- Invented anecdotes, numbers or reactions to make the post feel personal. Ask for the real one.

## Vietnamese notes

- The local forms of these tells: the confession arc and *Nói thẳng.* openers (VI-06), the split contrast *Không phải X. Mà là Y.* (VI-04), *Điều thú vị là / Cái hay ở đây là* (VI-05).
- Voice follows the writer's own posts: *mình* or *tôi*, particles and slang only at the rate their sample uses them (VI-42).
- English tech terms stay as the community writes them; no gloss for a technical audience.

## Review checklist

- Does the first line say something a reader could not have guessed?
- Is there at least one detail that came from the writer, and none that was invented?
- Would each post in a thread still make sense on its own?
- Do the emoji, hashtags and line breaks match the writer's own posts?
